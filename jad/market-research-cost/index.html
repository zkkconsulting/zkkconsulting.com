Pricing

What market research costs for a startup

Prices vary widely because scope varies widely. This page explains what moves the cost and lays out ZKK's prices and terms plainly.

Drivers

What drives the cost

Market research cost depends on a handful of things, and any quote you get reflects some combination of them.

  • Scope: how many questions, segments, geographies, and competitors are covered.
  • Depth: whether a source is cited from a summary or the underlying data is read and checked.
  • Deliverables: a short brief costs less than a full report with a source appendix.
  • Add-ons: extra analyses, decks, or visuals.
  • Modeling: a multi-year financial model takes real hours, especially when it must be editable and documented.
  • Data access: some industries have little public data, which means more work per finding.

None of these drivers is unusual. They explain why two quotes for the same topic can differ by several multiples, and why it helps to ask what is and is not included before comparing prices. A low number for a thin scope and a higher number for a documented, sourced report are not the same product.

Pricing models

Fixed price and hourly pricing

Researchers generally charge hourly or by fixed price. Hourly rates suit open-ended work where the scope truly cannot be known. The tradeoff is that the final cost is uncertain, which makes budgeting harder for a startup.

Fixed pricing sets the scope and the price before work starts. The risk of overrun sits with the researcher, and the cost to the client is known. The tradeoff is that the scope must be defined up front, and work outside it is quoted separately. ZKK uses fixed prices for everything, including custom scopes.

Neither model is better in every case. A fixed price tends to suit a startup that needs a defined output by a defined budget. Hourly work can suit a company with an ongoing internal team that needs flexible support. Ask any provider how scope changes are handled, whichever model they use.

Terms

Payment terms and what to expect

After scoping, we send a Statement of Work and a Stripe invoice for the 50% deposit. Work begins when both are complete. The remaining 50% is invoiced on completion. One round of revisions is included, and you have five business days after delivery to send consolidated feedback.

Timelines are set during scoping and depend on depth, add-ons, access to materials, and your deadlines, so we do not publish a fixed number. A mutual NDA is available. We provide research and analysis, not legal, regulatory, medical, tax, accounting, or investment advice.

Smaller engagements can be a sensible first step. A client may begin with Launchpad, then move to Growth once the first findings are in, and we are glad to scope it that way.

Choosing

Choosing a starting point

If you need to know who you compete with, Launchpad is usually enough. If you need numbers for an investor, Growth. If you need a financial model to plan against, Comprehensive. If you are unsure, a smaller engagement is often the better first step, and we will say so on the call. See services and pricing or book a discovery call.
