Medical Devices

FDA and medical device research

Technical and regulatory research for medical-device and healthcare-technology markets, grounded in public regulations, guidance documents, FDA databases, standards, and engineering context.

Pathway & classification research

  • Public FDA classification and product-code research
  • Predicate device and comparable-product research
  • 510(k), De Novo, and pathway research
  • Public information on typical testing and documentation requirements
  • Q-Submission background research and meeting-preparation support
  • Publicly available timeline and milestone research

Quality systems & documentation research

  • Public research on quality management systems and design controls
  • Documentation structure and organization support
  • Design History File and technical-file organization support
  • Research on risk management and verification and validation documentation
  • Labeling research and technical-documentation support
  • Literature and published-evidence reviews

Testing, standards & commercial landscape

  • Public research on biocompatibility, EMC, and electrical-safety requirements
  • Accredited laboratory and testing-landscape research
  • Standards research for a given device type
  • Reimbursement, coding, and coverage landscape research
  • Competitive and market research for device categories
  • Market sizing and financial modeling for device companies

Wording matters here. We research and organize publicly available information from regulations, guidance, standards, and databases. We do not issue regulatory opinions, determine classification or clearance outcomes, provide legal advice, or act as a regulatory agent or legal representative. Reimbursement and coding research reflects publicly available sources as of the report date and is not billing or coding guidance.

Electronics

FCC and electronics research

Technical, standards, and certification research for connected and radio-frequency products, so potential requirements, timing, and cost considerations are understood early.

Certification & compliance research

  • Public research on FCC rule parts relevant to a product type
  • Certification pathway research, including typical steps and documentation
  • Commonly reported compliance considerations, based on credible published sources
  • Test laboratory and accreditation landscape research
  • Standards research beyond FCC, where relevant

Electronics & product research

  • Electronics and connected-product market research
  • Component, module, and technology landscape research
  • Supply-base and vendor landscape research
  • Public-source feature comparisons across competing products
  • Technical-document organization support

Same boundary. This is engineering and regulatory research, not a legal determination of compliance. We do not certify products, issue compliance opinions, or represent clients before the FCC or another agency. Certification decisions rest with accredited bodies, and legal determinations should come from qualified counsel.

Engineering

Engineering context for commercial questions.

Both founders are engineers. That background helps us interpret technical documentation, understand product claims in context, and connect them to market and commercial research.

We work from the primary sources

Datasheets, standards documents, regulatory databases, patents, and published literature are core research inputs, not material handed off for someone else to interpret.

We understand technical feasibility in context

Engineering training helps us recognize when a product claim is technically demanding, physically constrained, or economically difficult to scale.

We turn research into usable visuals

Product renders, technical illustrations, exploded views, system diagrams, and short explainer animations are produced in-house. The same engagement can deliver both the research and the visuals that communicate it.

Visual Communication

When a picture does the work

Investor decks, grant applications, technical documentation, conference posters, and internal reviews are clearer when research is translated into visuals. Visual services can be added to an engagement or commissioned separately.

  • Product renders and concept visualizations
  • Technical illustrations and exploded views
  • System and process diagrams
  • Short explainer animations and motion graphics
  • Pitch deck figures, charts, and slide design
  • Document formatting and visual redesign
