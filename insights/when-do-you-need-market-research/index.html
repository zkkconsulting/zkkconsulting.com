When does a startup need market research?

Before a decision you can’t easily undo. Until then, talk to customers.

Book a free consultationSee pricing
Short answer

Research pays off before a decision you cannot easily undo

Last updated November 18, 2026

Market research is worth paying for when a decision is expensive to reverse and you are guessing at something you could check. Before that point it is usually too early. After it, the money is already spent.

Good times

Five moments when research is worth it

  • Before a raise. Investors will ask how big the market is and who else is in it. A sourced answer reads very differently from a guess.
  • Before you commit to a design. Changing a device after tooling or a regulatory submission costs far more than checking first.
  • Before you set a price. Pricing too low is hard to walk back with early customers.
  • Before entering a new market or segment. What worked with one buyer group often does not transfer.
  • When a partner or acquirer asks. A clear outside view can move a conversation that has stalled on assumptions.
Not yet

When it is too early

  • You do not yet know what problem the product solves or for whom. Talk to users first.
  • The decision is cheap to reverse, such as a landing page or a small test.
  • You already have the answer from customers who are paying you.

In those cases your time is better spent on customer conversations. We will tell you so on the free call.

Questions

Common questions

Is market research worth it for an early-stage startup?
Yes, when a costly decision is coming up, such as a raise, a design freeze or a price. Before that, customer conversations are usually a better use of time.
How long before a raise should I do it?
Allow a few weeks before you start pitching, so the numbers are ready for your deck and for investor questions.
Can I do part of it myself?
Yes. Many founders build the first list of competitors themselves and bring us in to check it, size the market and fill the gaps.
